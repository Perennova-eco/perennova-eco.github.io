What the homepage of France’s 20 largest cities weighs

Perennova barometer · edition 1 · measured on 2 October 2026. One method for all: we download the page and everything its HTML declares, then count bytes, requests and third-party hosts. No carbon estimate. Every figure links to its dated report. Repeated every month, so the trajectory can be read.

What the measurement shows

  • 17 pages measured out of 20; three sites refuse the measuring server (see below).
  • Median weight: 3,445 KB. The mean is 3,935 KB.
  • 12 pages out of 17 exceed 2 MB (2,048 KB). Only two stay under 1 MB: Le Havre (884 KB) and Grenoble (949 KB).
  • A 1-to-14 gap between the lightest (884 KB) and the heaviest (at least 12,584 KB, measurement cap reached).
  • 29% of all measured weight comes from lazy-loaded images (loading="lazy"). A browser only loads them on scroll: a first-screen-only measurement does not see them.
  • Median Perennova scale: 6 out of 20. These are our own published thresholds, not an official standard.

Three sites not measured

  • Rennes (metropole.rennes.fr): the site answers 403 to the measuring server.
  • Reims (www.reims.fr): the site serves the measuring server a 222-byte holding page (anti-bot check), not the homepage.
  • Saint-Denis (La Réunion) (www.saintdenis.re): the site answers 403 to the measuring server.

Refusing a declared bot is a legitimate operating choice. It is neither a fault nor a score: these three cities are simply not ranked.

Why these twenty cities

They are the twenty most populous communes of France (Insee, municipal populations as of 1 January 2023, list taken from Wikipedia). All exceed 50,000 inhabitants: the French law of 15 November 2021 (known as REEN) requires them to define a responsible digital strategy by 1 January 2025 (Maire-info, 5 September 2022, in French).

The weight of a homepage is not that strategy. It is a measurable, dated fact that can be compared from one month to the next.

Method, and what it does not say

  • The measuring server downloads the HTML, then the scripts, stylesheets, images (including lazy-loaded ones) and url() files that this HTML declares. Caps: 200 sub-resources, 12 MB, 60 seconds.
  • It does not run JavaScript. Whatever a script loads afterwards (carousels, maps, videos, trackers) is not counted. In a browser the page therefore weighs at least this much, often more.
  • The weight shown is for the whole page, not the first screen: lazy-loaded images are counted, and their share is shown.
  • It measures neither CPU, memory nor battery on the device, and does not judge design intent. It produces no carbon estimate.
  • One measurement per site, on a single day, with no cache or cookies, from a server that identifies itself as PerennovaScan. A homepage changes: the dated report holds for its date, not beyond.
  • “Third-party host”: any hostname different from the page’s, including a subdomain of the same organisation.
  • This barometer is not a compliance audit against the French RGESN guidelines.
